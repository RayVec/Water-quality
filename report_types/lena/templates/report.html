Learn About Your Report

LENA Background

This report describes the language and audio environment, which includes sounds, silence, speech, and other noises that the child can hear in the home environment of your child based on your LENA recording.

LENA Database

What is LENA Database

The LENA database is a group of 329 children's recordings collected by LENA to establish the norms for everyday language in young families.

WCWH uses the database to compare your child's recording to recordings in the database to identify your child's percentiles.

Recording Hours: 32,000+ hours
Participants: 329 children
Age Range: 2 to 48 months
Collection Context: Natural home environments

Whose Data is Included

The LENA database mostly represents white (66%), English-speaking children from the US. Therefore, your percentiles may not fully reflect the norms for multilingual families or families of color.

The LENA automatically detects speech in both English and Spanish, but the language and demographic makeup of the database can impact how well the results represent your child's experience.

Support Your Child's Language Growth

Ways you can do to support your child's language growth, improving their vocabulary/reading skills, brain structure/function, assessment scores, and social-emotional development.

14 Talking Tips developed by LENA

  1. Talk about what you're doing
  2. Comment on what they see or do
  3. Name things that interest them
  4. Get down to their level: face to face
  5. Touch, hug, hold
  6. Tune in and respond to what they notice
  7. Wait for their response
  8. Imitate them, and add words
  9. Make faces, use gestures
  10. Take turns – don't do all the talking
  11. Repeat and add on their words
  12. Follow their lead, do what interests them
  13. Encourage them, be positive
  14. Be silly! Relax and have fun!
